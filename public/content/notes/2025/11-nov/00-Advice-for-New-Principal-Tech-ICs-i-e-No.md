good read, I liked this one
- different flavors of principals, though focus on code will help you stay successful
- coding is now less impactful than your decision making
- you're a part time PM (PgM, hiring, etc.)
- more communication, influence, and connecting the right people
- being right is only half the battle - you need to convince others of your vision
- you need to teach your org what to value
- some work just can't happen without your attention
- you can only do so much yourself - 
  - Identify one or two ICs you’d like to groom and set goals for yourself on how you’ll help them
- transition to making work theirs vs involving others in your work
- when you give others the work, it is now theirs - you can guide, but the direction/drive is theirs
- create space for others in meetings
- you don't always need to demonstrate value - if things are working well, you can be quiet
- learn to guard your time
- if you can't explain why what you're working on needs a principal, you may be working on the wrong thing
- you get an aura of credibility even if you shouldn't have it
- share the why of what you're thinking, not just the what
- find mechanisms to stay in touch with teams (design reviews, periodic demos, etc)
  - keep your pulse on the org to find problems/opportunities
- help teams keep an eye on the bigger picture
- be pragmatic - accept local solutions balanced with big picture needs
- you can decline to give feedback on people you haven't worked much with (and you will be asked)
- make time to interact with interns and their mentors
- must go beyond the critical path to be effective as an IC6 - org shouldn't depend on you to succeed
- you get a lot of freedom, tied with responsibility to use it well
- define + align your charter with leadership
- it can get lonely :(
- don't neglect your own needs - make time/space for projects/your own growth
- keep learning - timebox projects where you're not learning
